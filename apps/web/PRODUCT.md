# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Garden primarily serves non-technical roles at small-to-mid companies—leaders, operations, finance, marketing, sales, and support—working alongside personal AI agents. Developers on the same teams use the same workspace for agent-authored work, issues, connectors, and approvals. Users need one dependable operating surface where human and agent work stays visible, attributable, and easy to continue.

## Product Purpose

Garden is an operating system where humans and persistent personal agents work side by side. It brings issues, chat, inbox, skills, connectors, permissions, and agent activity into one tabbed workspace. Success means non-technical users gain powerful agent workflows without losing control, while expert users retain the full capability ceiling. Useful workflows discovered by one teammate should become reusable workspace capability rather than private setup knowledge.

## Brand Personality

Warm, capable, calm. Garden should feel like a cultivated workshop at golden hour: lived-in and human, with precise product behavior beneath editorial restraint. It is a quiet room where people and agents do serious work—not a chatbot spectacle or an infrastructure console.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble a cold developer tool, dark AI laboratory, generic SaaS dashboard, or cartoon garden theme. Avoid midnight-indigo surfaces, bright crayola accents, hard square chrome, heavy drop shadows, pure-white page grounds, ornamental glass effects, and agent-fleet control-panel language. Familiar product affordances should remain familiar; visual personality must not make routine work strange.

## Design Principles

1. **Raise the floor without lowering the ceiling.** Give non-technical users clear rails while preserving expert capability and inspectability.
2. **Keep humans in control without creating approval fatigue.** Make agent state, permissions, ownership, and consequential actions legible at the moment they matter.
3. **Make shared capability compound.** Skills, context, and successful workflows should be reusable across the workspace rather than trapped with one person.
4. **Keep users in the work.** Prefer warm caches, persistent tabs, inline actions, optimistic updates, and continuity over navigation churn and loading screens.
5. **Earn trust through operational clarity.** Workspace, assignee, source, status, and active work must remain obvious; avoid hidden context switches or ambiguous state.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal conformance target is currently required. Still follow accessible product conventions: keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, readable contrast, reduced-motion support, adequate targets, semantic labels, and status cues that do not rely on color alone. Preserve familiar interaction patterns so occasional and non-technical users can operate the product confidently.
